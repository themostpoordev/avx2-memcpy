Kernel 8 — the memset ladder — analysis
=======================================

Rungs
-----
src/memset_naive.c     byte stores, nothing vectorised (SCALAR_SRCS)
src/memset_word.c      8 B store per iteration + byte tail (SCALAR_SRCS)
src/memset_sse2.c      16 B stores, 4x unrolled, forced 128-bit (SSE2_SRCS)
src/memset_avx2.c      32 B stores, 4x unrolled, overlapping < 32 B tail
src/memset_avx2_nt.c   32 B non-temporal stores above 1 MiB, with a dst-alignment
                       prologue, cached tail and one sfence
src/memset_asm.S       hand-written AVX2 fill, same shape as rung 4

Verify — PASS on znver2, znver3, v3 for all six (glibc reference, n = 0..4096 at
random offsets, 64 B canaries both sides, plus the ladder sizes). Gate — audit
and audit-width PASS. Widths as declared: 8 / 64 / 128 / 256 / 256 / 256 bits.

One defect, caught before timing: the first memset_asm encoding used
`vpbroadcastb %esi, %ymm0`, which the assembler accepts but encodes with an EVEX
(0x62) prefix — an AVX-512 instruction on AVX2-only targets. `--verify` died with
SIGILL on the first run. Fixed to `vmovd %esi,%xmm0` + `vpbroadcastb %xmm0,%ymm0`
(VEX), and the gate now fails any object containing a 0x62-prefixed instruction,
so the class of defect is caught before a timing row exists.

Campaign
--------
Tag k8 (2026-10-20 07:23:05 – 07:35:18), `OP=memset`, 5 reps x 9 windows,
taskset -c 2, iters 4000, 0 migrated reps, 13 sizes, baseline glibc.
Tables: results/phase2_6_k8_<target>.matrix.txt.
Rung-vs-rung tiers: results/phase2_6_k8_pairs.txt (scripts/mo_analyze.sh).

The null band, measured for memset as well. `scripts/mo_analyze.sh` prints n/a
because it keys the band on the impls named `naive`/`ref_scalar`, and `naive` has
no memset function; but `memset_naive` and `memset_ref` ARE the memset twin pair —
two TUs holding the same byte-store loop. Computed from the matrix files in these
same windows, |memset_naive - ref_scalar| is **0.00% at every size from 16 B to
1 MiB** and 0.42% at 4 MiB (worst of the three targets). So the band-aware floor
is max(1.5%, ~0) = 1.5% for every memset key, and the mandated Tier-A floor is
binding. That is a finding in itself: fills are far more repeatable than copies
here (no read stream, no cache-state dependence), and the 1-4% deltas below are
real claims rather than band noise — unlike the memcpy ladder, where the band at
16-64 B was 2.4-4.5%.

Crossovers and who wins where
-----------------------------
  - Below 256 B, **glibc wins** and it wins with a flat 2.5 ns: its small-fill is
    one broadcast plus one or two stores and almost no dispatch. Our best rung
    (asm) is 2.8 / 2.5 / 2.8 / 3.1 ns at 16/32/64/128 B — within a step at 32 B,
    one step behind elsewhere.
  - **256 B is the first size where a rung matches glibc** (asm 3.4 = glibc 3.4).
  - From 512 B to 64 KiB the asm rung is level with glibc (5.6 vs 5.5, 10.6 vs
    10.3, 40.7 vs 40.1, 167.8 vs 165.2, 641 vs 651) and the C avx2 rung converges
    to it at 1 KiB and above.
  - 1 MiB and 4 MiB are level (14989 vs 14998; 65112 vs 64902), both Tier A.

The two results worth carrying forward
--------------------------------------
1. **Dispatch depth decides the small fills, exactly as it did for memcpy.**
   The C AVX2 rung is 4.7 ns at 32 B while its own hand-written twin is 2.5 ns
   with the same stores; the difference is the branch chain into the tail
   (`cmp 0x80 / jae`, `xor`, `jmp`, then the 32 B loop's preheader) versus two
   compares in the asm. This is the same mechanism V9b/F4 attacked in the memcpy
   kernel, seen from the other side, and it is why the asm rung is the best of the
   ladder below 512 B.
2. **Non-temporal stores are a trap for a cache-resident fill.** Above the 1 MiB
   gate the NT rung is 3.3x slower at 1 MiB and 3.2x slower at 4 MiB, because the
   harness reuses one buffer: it lives in the 8 MiB L3, and NT stores bypass the
   cache and push every line to DRAM (50.1 us vs 15.0 us at 1 MiB = ~21 GB/s of
   DRAM write instead of L3-resident stores). NT for a fill can only win when the
   destination is genuinely streaming and larger than the last-level cache, which
   this harness's working set is not. The rung is kept as evidence, not adopted.

Verdicts
--------
  naive / word   ladder floor, superseded. word is a certified +72…+88% over
                 naive everywhere above 32 B and is the last rung that is
                 scalar-only.
  sse2           certified win over word from 64 B upward; superseded by avx2
                 above 512 B.
  avx2           certified win over sse2 from 512 B upward and level with glibc
                 from 1 KiB up, but **certified losses at 32-256 B** against its
                 own sse2 rung. A width step that costs 4 sizes at the bottom.
  asm            **best rung of the ladder**: matches glibc at 256 B and from
                 512 B to 64 KiB, level at 1 MiB and 4 MiB, and beats the C rung
                 by 28-47% at 32-512 B. This is the memset rung worth keeping.
  avx2_nt        **rejected**: 3.2-3.3x slower than cached stores at and above
                 its own gate because the destination is cache-resident.

Limitations
-----------
  - The memset band is 0.00% (see above), so the 1.5% floor is binding and small
    deltas are claims. That is stricter than the memcpy ladder, where a 2% delta
    at 32 B would be inside the band and not a claim.
  - The memset `naive` and `ref_scalar` implementations are the only rungs whose
    memset functions are byte loops; the memcpy-side `naive`/`ref_scalar` rows
    still exist in the same binaries, so the CAMPAIGN's impl list is the same
    length but two of them contribute no memset function. The matrix says which
    (they are absent from the memset rows).
  - 4 MiB rows are Tier B and are not claims; nothing above depends on them.
