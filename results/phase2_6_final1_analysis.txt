Phase 2.6 campaign "final1" - analysis (V6 flags, V7 PGO, V9 fused kernel)
=========================================================================

What was measured
-----------------
Tag final1, 2026-10-16 18:59:26 -> 19:15:51, three targets (znver2, znver3, v3),
5 reps x 9 windows (RUN_TO_CAP=1), 13-point ladder, taskset -c 2, iters 4000.
Driver: scripts/phase2_6_run.sh; raw logs results/phase2_6_final1_<target>.windows.log;
tables results/phase2_6_final1_<target>.matrix.txt (scripts/phase2_6_matrix.sh,
baseline glibc). 0 migrated reps were dropped in any window.

The campaign ran PROBE=0 on the normal binaries except that the two link-time
rungs (-flto, PGO) are rows of bench/<t>_perf6, so all twelve rungs were measured
inside ONE binary per target: the -flto and PGO objects sit next to their
non-flagged twins in the same process, in the same windows (protocol rule 4).
That is the honest control for a flag: the only difference between
avx2_final_gcc and avx2_final_pgo is how that one object was compiled.

Impls: ref_scalar, naive, glibc, avx2_final (clang), avx2_final_gcc,
avx2_final16_gcc, avx2_final_gcc_ofast, avx2_final_gcc_unroll,
avx2_final_gcc_noplt, avx2_final_noomit, avx2_final_lto, avx2_final_pgo.

Read this first: the null band
-----------------------------
The band column is |naive vs ref_scalar| at that size - two translation units
holding the same byte loop with byte-identical instruction streams. It is the
measurement artefact, not a result, and it is large at exactly the sizes where
the AVX2 rungs are fast: 4.35% at 16 B, 9.51% at 64 B, 20.22% at 4 KB. Tier A
needs a per-key spread <= 10%; most keys here are Tier B (25% flat floor), which
means the protocol cannot certify anything smaller than 25% at those sizes. That
is why so many rows below read "measured" rather than WIN/LOSS - it is the
protocol refusing a claim, not a missing measurement. Nothing in this file
upgrades a "measured" row to a win in prose.

Verdicts
--------
V6, compiler flags on the adopted compiler (gcc). Each rung is avx2_final_gcc
plus exactly one flag, and the pair deltas (B faster than A, per target) are:

  -Ofast           0.00% at every size from 16 B to 512 B and at 64 KB on all
                   three targets; the only movement is +-4% at 4 MiB, where the
                   spread is 25% (Tier B). It compiles the same bytes. REJECTED:
                   no measurable gain, and it licenses FP reassociation the
                   kernel does not want.
  -funroll-loops   0.00% or small LOSSES: -19.35% at 16 B, -10.71% at 32 B,
                   -5.12% at 1 MiB (worst case over the three targets).
                   REJECTED - it actively hurts the tiny class.
  -fno-plt         0.00% at 16..512 B; +3.07% at 1 MiB and +15.15% at 4 MiB,
                   both inside Tier B where 25% is the floor. The control rung
                   for a kernel that makes no calls: nothing to see. REJECTED
                   (not demonstrably useful; keeps PLT indirection for callers).
  -fno-omit-frame- 0.00% at 16..512 B, -3.55% at 4 MiB. REJECTED: the flag is
   pointer         already on in OPT for every rung, and forcing it off is not
                   faster anywhere, so it buys nothing - but the experiment was
                   worth running, because "keeps a register" was the hypothesis.
  -flto            0.00% at most sizes, but -9.68% at 16 B, -6.25% at 128 B,
                   and -21.43% at 32 B on v3 against +9.68% on znver3 (the sign
                   flips across targets, so it is noise, not a gain). One
                   certified win: 512 B on znver2 (+15.71%, Tier A). A single
                   win at one target out of three does not adopt a link-time
                   build requirement. REJECTED for the deliverable; the rung
                   stays in the probe binary as the record.

  Short version: no compiler flag earned its place. The fused kernel keeps the
  plain gcc flag set, and V1's gcc adoption is the only build change that
  survived.

V7, PGO (-fprofile-generate over the size ladder, then -fprofile-use). The only
change in the whole campaign that moved the tiny class in the right direction,
on all three targets and in the same direction:

  16 B    1.9 ns vs 3.1 ns for the same source without the profile (+25% over
          glibc, certified Tier A on znver2 and znver3, Tier B at the floor on v3)
  32 B    1.9 ns vs 3.1 ns (+25.1/+25.2/+25.5%, certified Tier A on all three)
  64 B    2.8 ns vs 3.1 ns (-12.6% vs glibc, still a loss, unchanged verdict)
  128 B   2.8 ns vs 3.3 ns (the one size where PGO buys a tie with glibc)
  256 B..4 MiB  unchanged - the profile does not touch the loop.

  What it means: the 25% loss at 16-64 B was never in the copies. PGO's only
  relevant action here is to move the cold size classes out of the hot path,
  which is a statement about the ENTRY CHAIN (six compares before the tiny case
  is reached), not about code generation quality. PGO is NOT adopted: it needs a
  profile build per target, and it is the wrong fix for the right diagnosis -
  see V9b, which does the same thing statically.

V9, the fused kernel (clang build avx2_final, gcc build avx2_final_gcc). The
knobs are V1 (gcc), V3 (32 B-aligned main loop), V4 (unroll 4), V5 (prefetch
+128 B, only at 64 KB and up), V8 (flat overlap classes, no tail branch).

  Certified: 512 B +16.83% on znver2 (clang) and on v3 (gcc and final16_gcc).
  Measured but below the floor: +8.4% at 256 B, +15.7..17.0% at 512 B on the
  remaining target/rung pairs, +7.9..10.8% at 1 MiB. 4 MiB is unusable in this
  campaign (spread 25%, and the clang and gcc builds of the same knobs differ by
  10 points in opposite directions - that is the window, not the code).
  Certified LOSS: 16 B (-25.1%, Tier A, all three targets), 32/64 B (-25%/
  -12.5%, mostly Tier B).

  So the honest V9 verdict: the fused kernel is level with glibc from 1 KB to
  64 KB, faster at 256 B and 512 B, faster at 1 MiB by ~8% (uncertified), and
  25% slower at 16-32 B. The tiny class is the one real regression, and V9b
  exists to price its cause.

V9b (campaign in flight, tag v9b): avx2_final_v2_gcc - identical to
avx2_final_gcc except TUNED_SMALL_PATH 1, which takes n <= 64 from one compare
and copies kernel 4's measured small shape. Control is avx2_final_gcc in the same
windows; the prediction from V7 is 16/32 B at ~1.9-2.2 ns and no movement at
256 B..4 MiB. Results go in the consolidated report, not here.
